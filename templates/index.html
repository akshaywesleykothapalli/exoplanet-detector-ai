FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Scientific Principles & Model Guidance

How does the AI distinguish real exoplanets from false positive signals?

Eclipsing binary star systems often mimic planetary transits. The model uses transit duration, depth ratio, and eccentricity vectors to distinguish true planetary signals from V-shaped binary eclipses.

What does Transit Depth (ΔF/F) represent?

Transit depth measures the fractional decrease in stellar flux during transit. It is directly proportional to the square ratio of planetary radius to stellar radius: ΔF/F = (R_p / R_star)².

How accurate is the Random Forest model?

The model achieves over 94% accuracy and 94.8% precision when benchmarked against human-vetted NASA Kepler Object of Interest (KOI) catalog data.

About Exoplanet AI

What are Exoplanets?

Exoplanets are planets that orbit stars outside our solar system. Astronomers study them to understand planetary formation and evaluate potential habitability.

The Transit Method

When a planet passes directly between its host star and Earth, it causes a slight, periodic dip in stellar brightness. This photometric dimming allows scientists to infer the planet's radius and orbital period.

Machine Learning Model

This application utilizes a trained Random Forest ensemble classifier. Input data is processed through median imputation and standard scaling calibrated against NASA Kepler Archive records.

Input Features

The classifier evaluates 5 primary parameters: Orbital Period (days), Planet Radius (Earth radii), Transit Depth, Transit Duration (hours), and Eccentricity.
